# Perspective: Wanting

Wanting is the fourth colour of Perspective, the bottom right arrow of the Variable. Contemporary teaching describes it as a view oriented outward toward other people. A guide to the Variables in this library puts it in two sentences: "Your focus is on others and community needs. You have a natural inclination towards activism and addressing societal issues." A contemporary lecture on the Perspective arrow describes the same colour as "the one that resonates with needs", adding that "this one sees the other. This is the community aspect." What such a person notices first, in this account, is "the needs of others" and "the needs of the many".

That is very nearly the whole of what the source material in this library says about Perspective colour 4. It is the thinnest of the six views by a wide margin, and the reason is largely an accident of vocabulary. The word "wanting" is common in ordinary English and it is also the word Ra Uru Hu used for a different Variable slot entirely. Anyone searching the recorded material for it will surface almost nothing about this view and a great deal about something else.

## Mechanics

Perspective is derived from the COLOUR of the Personality Nodes. Ra Uru Hu described that position plainly: "the personality nodes represent the fact that each and every one of us have a unique and differentiated way in which we look at the world. It is called perspective and it is our unique view."

Wanting is colour 4 in the canonical sequence of six: Survival, Possibility, Power, Wanting, Probability, Personal. Sitting fourth places it in the upper half of the sequence, the transpersonal half, which fits the outward orientation contemporary teaching gives it.

The arrow that carries Perspective points either left or right, and that direction is set by TONE, not by colour. Left is Focused and right is Peripheral. There is no left and right variant name attached to Wanting itself, or to any of the other five Perspective colours, in the material available here. A chart showing Wanting will show it as either Focused or Peripheral, and that pairing comes from the tone beneath the colour.

## The naming trap

Ra Uru Hu used the word "wanting" for Motivation colour 4, not for Perspective colour 4. Discussing what happens to a first colour Motivation under conditioning, he said: "the motivation of the four is what it wants. It wants something. And because it's not backed by total intelligence, it does not do it very well. It doesn't really know. So, it's motivated to want without knowing what it wants."

Contemporary teaching resolved the collision the other way round. It gives Motivation colour 4 the name Need, and reserves Wanting for Perspective colour 4. The result is that one word names two different things depending on which body of material you are reading. A reader who searches Ra's recorded lectures for "wanting" will land on Motivation, on the mind and its confusion, and not on this view at all. See [[Motivation: Need]] for that slot.

The contemporary Perspective lecture in this library does not use the word Wanting either. It calls the fourth view social and describes it through need. The name Wanting for this view appears here in only one written contemporary guide.

## What the view is said to see

The outward orientation is the one consistent claim. Contemporary teaching describes someone who registers what a group requires before registering what they themselves require, and who may act on that by building or organising something that fills the gap. The same source allows that the perception can be turned to personal advantage, noting that you "might see what the community needs and you go and fill that need by creating something", but treats the more characteristic expression as heart centred, and names activism as a place this view tends to show up.

This stays at the level of perception rather than character. The Genetic Matrix reference in this library is blunt that Variable "is not a personality label" and that left and right orientations "are not value judgments". Nothing here promises that a Wanting perspective makes a person generous, and the contemporary lecture warns that having a view is not the same as being good at using it.

## Distraction toward Survival

Under conditioning, a Perspective slips along the harmonic 1 to 4, 2 to 5, 3 to 6 and back. Ra Uru Hu called this movement distraction rather than transference, reserving transference for the Motivation side. His description: "So if you're not correct and you're not operating according to your strategy and authority and you're making decisions with your mind instead of having the perspective you're supposed to have, you get the harmonic. You get distracted. You get distracted by what you're not."

On that harmonic, the distraction partner of Wanting is [[Perspective: Survival]], colour 1, the analytical view that looks for patterns and for what can be counted on. Ra only ever spelled out one of the three Perspective pairs, Possibility and Probability, and once mentioned his own, Personal distracted by Power. The Survival and Wanting pair is not quoted anywhere in this library. It follows the harmonic Ra stated, and it should be read as that and not as recorded teaching.

## Why environment comes first

Perspective is the third of the four transformations, and it does not function on its own. Ra Uru Hu: "Because if you're in the correct environment, then you're going to see what you're here to see." And, on what happens otherwise: "if the mind does not have access to correct perspective, then the mind can never, ever, ever, ever, ever be correct. And you cannot have the correct perspective if you're not in the right place."

A Wanting view is therefore not a trait to be cultivated by deciding to care about other people. It is a perception that becomes available downstream of a correct dietary regimen and a correct [[Environment]], and upstream of conceptualising. Variable is not a shortcut past Type, Strategy and Authority.

## What the corpus does not support

There is no Ra Uru Hu material in this library describing Perspective colour 4 specifically. There are no left and right variants for it, no not-self description, no exalted and detriment reading, and no account of how it pairs with particular Motivations or environments. Any such detail found elsewhere is outside what can be sourced here.
