# Determination: Taste

Taste is the second colour of [[Determination]], the digestive theme carried by the colour of the Design Sun and Earth. Where the first colour, [[Determination: Appetite|Appetite]], concerns taking food in one thing at a time, the second concerns how narrow the field of food is in the first place. Ra Uru Hu, working through the six colours in his recorded Nutrition lecture, puts it bluntly: "the two is incredibly incredibly selective." He says the word twice, then again on its own.

What follows from that selectivity is the most distinctive claim made about this colour anywhere in the recorded material. Ra: "these are beings that are intended to have a very precise and set food regimen. That is a menu, a set menu that they repeat every single day." The only movement permitted in that menu is seasonal, "a menu that can only change in relationship to seasons," which is to say the shift in what is actually available through the year rather than a shift in appetite or curiosity. He nicknames the type the "desert island people" and frames the question that way: what one wonderful meal would you happily eat three times a day?

The name Taste can mislead a first reading. It does not mean a refined palate, a sophisticated sense of flavour, or an interest in food as a subject. It describes a digestive system that recognises a small set of things it can process and does badly with anything outside that set. Ra's summary is plain: "It recognizes what it likes and it has to stay with it."

## Mechanics

Determination is the colour of the Design Sun and Earth, and it is the first of the four arrows, sitting top left on the body side of the chart. Colour gives the category. The tone beneath the colour gives the binary, the left or right variant, and it is the tone's position that makes the arrow point one way or the other. As Ra says of the six regimens in the Nutrition lecture, "those regimens are dualities. In other words, that they have two sides to them that binary is the influence of the tone underneath the color." Six colours with two sides each is why he elsewhere counts "12 kinds of ways in which human beings are designed to digest their food."

Taste is colour 2 of 6. That places it in the lower half of the Determination scale, and Ra gives the lower half a shared keynote. Of the graph of six he says: "The bottom three have a super key note. And that super key note that they have is called conditions. So each of the lower colors, the one, two, and the three, they all bring certain eating conditions. The upper color brings circumstances." Appetite, Taste and [[Determination: Thirst|Thirst]] are therefore conditions, rules about the intake itself. [[Determination: Touch|Touch]], [[Determination: Sound|Sound]] and [[Determination: Light|Light]] are circumstances, conditions of the setting around the intake.

Ra treats the ascent from one to six as an evolutionary sequence rather than a ranking. Colour 1 is "a very, very old digestive system" that "isn't able to handle a lot of complexity," and colour 2 sits one step along from that but is still described as "a primitive system," "an older digestive system that isn't capable of dealing with a huge array of different varieties of foods going into the system." He is explicit elsewhere that the more elaborate circumstance systems are "not better. There's no better in any of this."

## Open and Closed

The two variants of Taste are named Open and Closed. Neither name appears in Ra's recorded Nutrition material in this library, where he works through the colour by number and describes the whole of the two as selective. Both variant names come from contemporary teaching, and the two contemporary sources here do not quite agree with each other.

One describes Open Taste as "benefiting from variety and experimentation" and Closed Taste as "thriving on a consistent, familiar diet." The other is narrower and reads more consistently with Ra: left tone Taste is Open, "gradually expanding its menu to new tastes over time," while right tone Taste is Closed, "born with a zipper, sticking to familiar foods and resisting new tastes," with the illustration of the child who wants spaghetti at every meal.

The difference matters. On Ra's account the whole of colour 2 is built around repetition, so an Open variant that amounts to free variety would contradict the colour it belongs to. The second description, gradual expansion of a set menu rather than openness to anything, keeps the selectivity intact and only changes the rate at which the menu is allowed to grow. Where the sources conflict, this article presents both and notes that only the second sits comfortably inside Ra's description of the colour.

## Not the same as Cognition: Taste

There is a second thing in Variable called Taste, and it is not this. [[Cognition: Taste]] is tone 2, not colour 2. Cognition sits in the layer beneath colour, and it describes a mode of knowing, one of the six senses through which a person takes in and processes the world, paired with Smell in the splenic binary. Determination Taste is a dietary condition read off colour and tells you nothing about how a person cognises. The two are independent: a chart can carry colour 2 with any of the six tones underneath it, so Determination Taste and Cognition Taste coincide only by coincidence.

The numbering does rhyme, and Ra notices the rhyme without collapsing the two. "Remember that color and tone have a direct harmonic and resonating relationship to each other," he says, and the first two colours are "related to, in the sense we understand it in design, the way in which awareness awakens through the Splenic system, in the language and the taste of instinct and judgment." Colour itself, he adds, "is based on the same numerical construct as tone and line." The layers echo one another. They are still different layers, answering different questions.

## Resonance, harmony and the pull toward Sound

Because colour, tone and line share the same construct of six, each colour resonates with the profile line of the same number. Taste resonates with [[line 2]], and harmonises with [[line 5]]. Ra describes the harmonic directly at the colour level: "there is a harmonic relationship that exists between the first and the fourth color, the second and the fifth color, and the third and the sixth."

That harmonic is also the route of transference. Transference is what happens when the magnetic monopole fails to capture the emitted colour frequency cleanly. Ra: "Color is something that transfers and more than anything else it is this transference of color. The inability of the monopole to capture it correctly. It is this term transference that is at the very root of what I refer to as the homogenized being." The colour that is emitted is always perfect; the capture is what goes wrong, and when it goes wrong it slips along the harmonic and lands on a value that is "not substantiated by the tonal values underneath it."

For Taste the harmonic partner is colour 5, [[Determination: Sound|Sound]]. Ra describes Sound as the circumstance of acoustic environment, "High frequency, low frequency, loud, soft, there, not sound." Note what the slippage crosses: Taste is a condition, a rule about the food itself, while Sound is a circumstance, a rule about the setting. The corpus states the harmonic and the mechanism of transference clearly but does not describe what the 2 to 5 slippage looks like in a lived Determination, so that gap is left open here rather than filled in.

## Under conditioning

The lived difficulty of this colour is social and it starts early. Ra's example is the infant: "these are babies that are forced to eat diversity. All they want is the same plum sauce over and over again, and their mother is sticking tuna in them and this in them and that in them, and they are really fixed on one thing and they spit it out and the mother sticks it back in their mouth because it is not commonly understood."

The resistance continues into adulthood, and Ra locates it in the mind rather than the body. Many twos already recognise the trait, that they love the same breakfast or the same kind of lunch, but balk at fixing it deliberately: "the idea that they have to fix it, you know, it's psychological. It's mental thing." He also names the outside view honestly. "To anybody other than a two, that would seem horrendously redundant, repetitive, boring."

Against that he sets the general rule of the whole first transformation. Correctness here is not about quality of food but about the manner of intake. "It's not about what you are eating," he says, "it is about the conditions or the circumstances in which you eat," and again, "It's not about how well you eat. Remember that. It's how correctly you eat." Dietary regimen is the first of [[The Four Transformations]], and it follows [[Strategy]] and inner authority rather than replacing them.
