# Cognition: Outer Vision

Outer Vision is the third of the six tones and the first half of the ajna binary. The first two tones sit in the splenic binary and take the world in through smell and taste. The third moves up into the ajna, and takes it in visually: eyes forward, locked on a thing, building a dimensional field around whatever has been found.

It is also the last tone on the left side of the tonal triangle, and Ra Uru Hu treats that position as the culmination of everything the left side is for. Describing the climb up the left: "when you finish the left and you get to the Azna center and you get to the three, well, you really get to the epitome of what it is to be strategic. That is this is the strategic outer vision."

Outer Vision is not a preference for looking at things, and it is not good eyesight. It is a cognitive architecture well beneath the surface of the chart, and whether its plan reaches the surface is a question Ra ties to living out strategy and authority.

## Mechanics

Tone is a layer of the substructure. Beneath a gate is a line, beneath the line colour, and beneath colour six tones. Tone is what Ra calls cognition, and sensory potential is read through the Sun and Earth configuration: "When we are looking at sensory potential at deeper levels we are looking at it through the sun/earth configuration."

Tone is always drawn as a triangle, and Ra notes that the triangle mirrors the BodyGraph itself. The six tones sit in that triangle in three pairs: "We have the splenic binary on the lower left tone one and two. We have the azna binary at the top of the triangle tone three and four. And then we have the solar plex binary at the lower right."

Tone also decides direction. Ra: "It's tone that establishes whether something is to the left or something is to the right." Tones one, two and three orient left; tones four, five and six orient right. Unlike the Determination and Environment values, the sources give no left and right variant names for the individual cognitions, so there is no pair of sub-labels under Outer Vision.

## The Last Tone on the Left

Because the split in the triangle falls between the third and fourth tones, Outer Vision occupies the highest position on the left. Ra describes the left as strategic throughout: "The left side is all strategic." He notes where its weight sits: "Twothirds of it is in the splenic binary." The first two tones are alertness and sensitivity, survival intelligence inspired by fear. The third is where that intelligence stops being defensive and becomes deliberate. It plans.

The third tone is therefore the most strategic point of the whole tonal triangle, the place where strategy reaches its finished form immediately before the architecture changes sides. The Genetic Matrix source is explicit that this is not a ranking of people: "These are not value judgments."

## Binocular Vision

Outer Vision is rooted in a physical mutation Ra returns to often, the migration of the eyes from the side of the skull to the front. "What it allows us to do is that it allows us to be able to pinpoint objects in a relative space. In other words, a visual dimensionality."

From that dimensionality comes everything the left side does with the world. "The Binocular vision looks at the world, focuses on things, creates its dimensional environment, establishes its strategy and controls it." Ra puts the mode of attention concretely: a left-oriented being watching a crowd finds a single point of focus, and because there is a single line of focus, it can be brought back in and stored as a memory, which becomes the key to the whole background. Focus, dimension, plan and a picture of the field that can be shared with another member of the species arrive as one package, and Ra treats that package as the reason weak and slow creatures came to dominate stronger ones.

## The Crossing Point

The tonal triangle is divided down the centre, and the line falls directly through the ajna binary, cutting it in half. This is where Ra locates the mutation in the visual cortex. Crossing it changes everything: "the moment that you go from the left side to the right side, you have this transition that takes place from the strategic active, the active body, the strategic left mind to the other side, the passive right body, the receptive right mind."

That crossing is the boundary between tone three and tone four. "You have this movement where you go from the outer vision of the third tone, this focused outer vision to the inner vision of the four." Elsewhere Ra calls it "an enormous step, this transition between outer and inner vision, the difference between what is called left and right."

This is where an arrow flips. Since tone sets orientation, an activation carrying the third tone sends its arrow left and the same activation carrying the fourth tone sends it right. Outer Vision is the last position before that reversal. See [[Cognition: Inner Vision]] for the other half of the binary.

## The Not-Self Mind

Ra attaches a warning to the third tone that he attaches to no other cognition, directly after the passage above: "This is the birth of the notself mind. This is where the notself mind comes from." The seven-centered being could see, focus, control and strategise, assumed on that basis that its mind should run its life, and the [[Not-Self]] followed.

This is a statement about the tonal root of the strategic mind in general, not a verdict on anyone carrying tone three. Ra says of leftness: "I'm not suggesting that if you're left-minded today, you're that." He adds that he has a strategic mind himself. The point is access: beings who do not follow strategy and authority "cut themsel off from their unique cognition."

## How It Is Commonly Misread

The most common confusion is between tonal vision and the eyes as they appear in the BodyGraph. Ra flags it himself when teaching the surface right eye and left eye system: "don't be confused here because the right eye has its root in left architecture, and the left eye has its root in right architecture." On the surface, "the right eye is always focusing; the left eye is always peripheral." The naming inverts, so reading surface eye labels back into the tone gives the wrong answer.

The second misreading is treating Outer Vision as an arrow. Cognition is not a fifth arrow but the layer underneath the four.

One note on the record. Ra's recorded material here describes Outer Vision almost entirely at the level of species and architecture, in one lecture on the substructure and one on the senses. It does not offer the practical individual keynote that exists for the Determination values, and that gap is in the sources rather than in the reading of them.
