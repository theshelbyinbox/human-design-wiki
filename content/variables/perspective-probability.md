# Perspective: Probability

Probability is the fifth colour of Perspective, the bottom right arrow. It is a way of looking at the world in which the dominant thing seen is what is likely, workable and realistic. Where another view fastens on the unrealised potential in a situation, this one fastens on the odds. It asks whether the thing in front of it can be done.

It is one of only two Perspective colours Ra Uru Hu names outright in the recorded material in this library, and he names it as half of a pair: "two of the very interesting views of the six views are a harmonic possibility and probability." Almost everything he says about Probability is said through its relationship to Possibility, the second colour, which sits opposite it on the harmonic. This article follows that structure.

Perspective is not a talent and not an opinion. It is the frame through which the personality takes in the world, and what comes through it is the raw material the mind works on. Ra: "it is here in your view that you establish what your mind is going to process."

## Mechanics

Perspective is derived from the COLOUR of the Personality Nodes. Colour is the layer beneath gate and line, and it carries a different name in each position: Determination on the design side, Motivation at the Personality Sun and Earth, Perspective at the Personality Nodes. The six Perspective colours in canonical order are Survival, Possibility, Power, Wanting, Probability and Personal. Probability is colour 5, in the upper half of the sequence.

One naming collision is worth flagging. Ra calls Motivation colour 4 "wanting", while contemporary teaching calls Motivation 4 "Need" and reserves "Wanting" for Perspective colour 4, the article at [[Perspective: Wanting]]. The same word names two different slots depending on the source.

Whether the arrow points left or right is not decided by the colour but by the TONE underneath it, the [[Cognition]] layer. Ra: "It is tone that establishes whether something is to the left or something is to the right." Left on this arrow is focused, right is peripheral. Unlike some Determination and Environment values, the Perspective colours have no left and right variant names in the corpus, so there is no named "focused Probability" or "peripheral Probability". There is only the colour and the direction of the arrow carrying it.

## Seeing What Is Likely

The clearest account of what this view looks at comes from the contrast Ra draws with Possibility. Of the second colour he says the correct perspective is "simply about seeing possibilities," and "is not about looking to see whether the possibilities are going to become something or where the possibilities are going to flourish." He then adds, of that view, "In that, there is nothing to bet on."

Probability is the view where there is something to bet on. In Ra's Holistic Analysis 2 material, the section on the fifth colour is titled "Probability: The Assumption That the Future is Going to Prove the Present." That is the mechanism in one line: the view reads the present situation as evidence about what the future will do with it.

Contemporary teaching calls this the realist view: seeing the world "in practical, probable terms," with a strength in "identifying useful and feasible solutions to challenges." A longer treatment describes the fifth perspective as "seeing what's probable," explicitly "not potential, not possible," and "not aspirational" but "super practical," and notes the consequence in groups, where this view moves what it believes can be moved and is equally capable of shutting down what it judges cannot be. That is later teaching, and it goes further than anything Ra himself says.

## The Distraction With Possibility

Under conditioning, Perspective slips along its harmonic. Ra calls this transference at the Sun and Earth and distraction at the nodes. "You have a natural perspective, a natural correct view that gets distracted."

The harmonic pairs at the colour level match those at the line level. Ra: "there is a harmonic relationship that exists between the first and the fourth color, the second and the fifth color. and the third and the sixth." Colour 5 therefore has colour 2 as its harmonic, and the distraction of a Probability view is [[Perspective: Possibility|Possibility]].

The slippage runs both ways, which is easy to get backwards. Ra: "the person that's supposed to see possibilities thinks that they see probabilities. And the person who is supposed to see probabilities thinks that all of those are possibilities." The first half is a Possibility view pulled toward Probability; the second is a Probability view pulled toward Possibility. Neither colour is the aggressor. Each is pulled toward the other when the being is not correct, and Ra's summary is that "they get very very confused."

The cost is not confined to what is seen. Because the view feeds the conceptualising mind, distraction corrupts what the mind then does with it. Running from 2 toward 5, Ra describes the mind "building concepts on a probability that, in fact, is not a probability at all." Running the other way, a Probability view supplies its mind with open potential where it should be supplying an assessment of the odds.

Ra does not treat distraction as merely unfortunate: one heading in his Post Graduate Rave Psychology material reads "Distraction Gives Us Balance," and another warns that it is "More Important to Pay Attention to Environment Than Distraction."

## Environment Comes First

Correct seeing is not something this view can arrange for itself. It is downstream of the body. Ra: "There's a huge difference in your life when you are environmentally correct because it automatically establishes what color brings to the personality nodes, perspective, view, the way of seeing that is absolutely correct for you." Elsewhere he calls what follows from correct environment, relationships and diet "the possibility of the first mental transformation, perspective."

The order is fixed. Ra: "if the mind does not have access to correct perspective, then the mind can never, ever, ever, ever, ever be correct. And you cannot have the correct perspective if you're not in the right place. And if you're not in the right place, and you're not eating correctly, then there is no way that you're going to go through any of these transformations." Contemporary teaching puts it more briefly: if you are not in the right place, you will not see properly. See [[The Four Transformations]] and [[Environment]].

None of it comes before Type, Strategy and Authority, which are the catalyst. Ra: "first the catalyzation and then you enter into the variable itself." He places the maturing of Environment and Perspective around the Saturn return: "It's between 30 and 50 that you establish through the environment its partner perspective and in that develop the foundation for awareness."

## Resonance and Harmony

Colours resonate with profile lines by number, so colour 5 resonates with line 5 and harmonises with line 2, the same 2 and 5 relationship that governs the distraction. Ra describes the harmonic structurally at the line level: line 1 sits at the base of the lower trigram and line 4 at the base of the upper, "therefore, they have a relationship to each other," and the same holds for 2 and 5 and for 3 and 6. The colour level repeats the pattern. Beyond that numerical resonance the corpus links this colour to no keynote of the fifth line, and none should be assumed. See [[Lines Overview]].

## How It Is Commonly Misread

Three misreadings recur.

The first is treating Probability as a better or more grown up view than Possibility. The two are a harmonic pair, not a ranking, and the Genetic Matrix reference in this library states of Variable orientations that "these are not value judgments."

The second is treating the view as a skill that is automatically well developed. Contemporary teaching is explicit that a perspective is a potential rather than a guarantee, a skill to be honed, and that carrying it says nothing about how well it is used.

The third is confusing Perspective with Motivation. Both sit on the personality side and their six values carry themes that sound alike. Motivation drives how the mind conceptualises; Perspective determines what the mind is given to work on. A person can be motivated one way and see another, and in most charts the two differ.
