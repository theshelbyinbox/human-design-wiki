# Environment: Mountains

Mountains is the fourth of the six Environment values, and the first of the three that Ra Uru Hu grouped as landscape rather than hardscape. Where the first three environments describe enclosed, built, human-made settings, Mountains describes a relationship to height. The structural quality is elevation: being above a thing rather than inside it, and seeing it from that position.

Ra introduced the value by saying that his own two sons are Mountains, and he used a domestic example rather than a geographic one. He has a two-story house, and it would be "folly" to put those children in bedrooms on the main floor when there is an upper floor available. "They need to get to the high ground," he said, and then immediately removed the obvious misreading: "it doesn't mean they have to climb Everest. They can be on the second floor of the house." In a separate Ra.TV talk he put it as plainly as it can be put: "mountain beings are beings that need to be in high places."

This is not a preference or a personality trait. In Ra's framing Environment is a health issue, and the correct environment is what nourishes the body from the outside. He also warned against reading any environment as a location on a map. "Every single human being has a correct place," he said. "And when I say place, I don't mean a place on a map in that sense."

## Mechanics

Environment is the bottom left arrow of the four, on the body side of the design. It derives from the COLOR of the Design Nodes. Colour 4 in that position gives Mountains. Its number places it fourth in the sequence Caves, Markets, Kitchens, Mountains, Valleys, Shores, and colour 4 resonates with line 4 of the profile and harmonises with line 1.

Ra described the arrow's orientation as coming from underneath, from tone rather than colour. Colour sets the standard and tone produces the variant: "color establishes what the standard is and tone creates the nuance of that standard bringing it either to a this or a that." He confirmed that each of the six environments carries a pair of binary keynotes on the external side of the chart, though in the Orientation lecture he did not read the Mountains pair out.

## Hardscape and Landscape

The move from Kitchens to Mountains is the hinge of the Environment sequence. Ra: "the lower three are our hardscape and the upper three are called landscape." He described the hardscape as what built the modern world, and said that everything in the basic construct of our cities sits in the first three environments.

He drew the distinction in terms of specificity. The lower colours are "very very specific," while the upper colours are, in his word, "generalized." It is no longer the closed-in environment, the particular cave, the particular market, the particular kitchen, "but it is a much broader environmental phenomena."

Ra tied this explicitly to the same split in [[Determination]], where the lower three colours are conditions and the upper three are circumstances. The Environment arrow repeats that structure one arrow down. A Mountains person is not looking for a room; they are looking for a condition of the ground they are standing on.

## Height, Air and Overview

Ra gave one physiological hook for Mountains and only one: "mountain is about oxygen actually. You know the higher you go the less oxygen you take in." That is the whole of what the recorded material in this library says about the mechanism. Everything else he offered was positional. He reached for the English idiom of taking the high ground, "to take this position where you're looking down on everything," and said that this is where such people are supposed to be.

The scale is deliberately flexible. Ra noted that a person can be on the flattest plain and still be at a high point on that plain, or higher up in a building, and that this satisfies the requirement. When his children are unwell he keeps them upstairs, a few metres of elevation being enough to matter.

Contemporary teaching keynotes Mountains as elevation, overview and detachment, and describes the need as one for physical or metaphorical height in order to see clearly. Note that this contemporary keynote often uses the word perspective, which collides with the name of the Variable arrow [[Perspective]]. They are not the same thing, and a reader should keep them apart.

## Active and Passive

Mountains is one of the three Environment values whose left and right variants are attested in this corpus, alongside Valleys narrow and wide and Shores natural and artificial. The pair is Active and Passive.

The naming and the descriptions come from contemporary course material, not from Ra's recorded lectures here. That material describes Active Mountains as thriving on being high up and in reduced oxygen, on getting out of the trenches and up to higher ground, on crisp air, expansive viewpoints and high floors of buildings, and on a balance between community and retreat. It describes Passive Mountains as wanting the height in order to get away from others, to spend time alone relaxing into their own aura, and as less drawn to the physical exertion of getting up high than the Active variant is.

Ra's own framework supports the existence of such a pair, since he stated that binary keynotes attach to each environment and that tone decides which side of the pair applies. The particular words Active and Passive, and the content above, should be read as later teaching rather than as his.

## Perspective and the Saturn Return

Environment is the second of the four transformations, after dietary regimen and before perspective. Ra placed its activation at the first Saturn return: "It's between 30 and 50 that you establish through the environment its partner perspective and in that develop the foundation for awareness." Before 30, he said, environment is not yet the requirement, and he told parents not to feel guilty about children who are not in their correct setting.

The pairing is structural rather than thematic. The Design Nodes carry Environment and the Personality Nodes carry Perspective, and Ra described the two as linked through the nodal mechanism: "you cannot take advantage of the unique perspective if you're not in the right place looking at the right things." For Mountains this reads almost literally, but the sequence is the point, not the metaphor.
