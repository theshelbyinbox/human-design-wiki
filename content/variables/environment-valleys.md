# Environment: Valleys

Valleys is the fifth of the six Environment colours and the second of the three that Ra Uru Hu grouped together as landscape. Ra divided the six in half: "the lower three are our hardscape and the upper three are called landscape." The hardscape colours, Caves, Markets and Kitchens, name enclosed and constructed settings, and Ra said it was the hardscape "that created the world". The landscape colours are broader. As he put it, the upper colours are not "the closedin environment the specific specific cave, the specific market, the specific kitchen in that sense, but it is a much broader environmental phenomena." Valleys describes a position within that broader terrain: low ground, down among settlement and human activity rather than above it or set apart from it.

The distinguishing feature of Valleys in Ra's own account is not scenery but traffic. Valleys are where things pass through. "You know, valleys are places where information flows. It is through valleys that all transportation moves, activity." A valley body is correctly placed when it is inside that movement and watching it go by, not when it is removed from it. Ra's image for this is a person sitting on a bench on a busy street watching everybody walk past.

Like every Environment, Valleys is a matter of where the body is rather than of taste, and Ra treated it as a health issue that becomes essential only from roughly the age of thirty onward.

## Mechanics

Environment is one of the four arrows of Variable and derives from the colour of the Design Nodes. It sits in the bottom left position of the four arrows, on the body side of Variable, and it is the second of the four transformations, following [[PHS & Diet|dietary regimen]] and preceding [[Perspective]]. Valleys is colour 5 in the canonical sequence: 1 Caves, 2 Markets, 3 Kitchens, 4 Mountains, 5 Valleys, 6 Shores.

Ra called the nodes the independent variable, the mechanism through which the magnetic monopole holds a being together, and linked Environment directly to Perspective on that basis, since one arrow comes from the Design Nodes and the other from the Personality Nodes: "you cannot take advantage of the unique perspective if you're not in the right place looking at the right things."

Colours resonate with profile lines by number, so colour 5 resonates with line 5. One contemporary course notes the same affinity in passing, observing that people with fifth lines in their variables "are very sensitive to messaging as well because it's like the valleys."

The Environment arrow points left or right, and that direction is set by tone, not by colour. The names commonly attached to those directions, Observed for left and Observer for right, appear only in contemporary sources in this library. They are a separate mechanic from the Narrow and Wide pair below, although at least one contemporary teacher treats the two as the same thing.

## Flow, Movement and Exchange

Ra's fullest statement on colour 5 makes the valley a conduit: "And the valley is the way in which we exchange information." The consequence he drew is social as much as physical. A valley person, he said, is "more likely in your life socially to interact with people that are outside of what would be your normal grouping", whether that grouping is material, educational, cultural or racial, because valley people "tend to be in the places where there is this flow and movement of information" and so meet the most unusual outsiders.

This gives Valleys a different character from its landscape neighbours. [[Environment: Mountains|Mountains]] is elevation and overview, getting above the activity in order to see it. [[Environment: Shores|Shores]] is the edge where one environment becomes another. Valleys is being inside the exchange, on the low ground, at the level where the movement happens.

A contemporary guide in the library compresses the colour into "Community, civilization, and activity", describing people with this Environment as thriving in the heart of human activity and settlement and needing to be part of the flow of daily life.

## Narrow and Wide

Valleys carries a pair of variants, Narrow and Wide. The names appear in contemporary course material rather than in Ra's recorded material in this library, and should be attributed that way. The same sources give Mountains an Active and Passive pair and Shores a Natural and Artificial pair.

Contemporary teaching describes the two through proximity and acoustics. Narrow Valleys is described as ground level with sound close at hand, favouring private one to one conversation, taking in information audibly, and hearing life going on immediately nearby. Wide Valleys is described as the acoustics of a much larger space, where the sounds of activity carry from a distance without being on top of the person, the sound of a nearby highway, river or literal valley.

Sound is the organising idea throughout this variant material, and the acoustic emphasis is entirely contemporary. Ra's own treatment of colour 5 in this library is about the flow of information, transportation and activity, and never frames the colour in terms of sound.

## Reading Valleys Correctly

The name misleads in the same way several Variable names do. Ra was direct that the environments are not to be read as literal terrain. He allowed that Valleys "can also just be a valley if you know what I mean or the bottom floor. of the house or the basement for that matter", and said of the whole set that there are "so many ways in which each of these values can be looked at." The scale is not fixed either. Speaking of Mountains, he noted that height does not require Everest, only a high point relative to what is around it, and the same relativity applies at the low end.

Contemporary descriptions in the library are not consistent about distance. One places the valley person in the heart of settlement and daily interaction. Another, from the same teacher, has valleys close to town but "not right in the middle of them", with the noise and bustle "removed a bit", suburban rather than downtown. Ra's account sits closer to the first, with the person down among the movement rather than at its margin. Some of the spread may be accounted for by the Narrow and Wide split, one variant inside the activity and the other taking it in from further off, but the corpus does not say so outright.

A second misreading worth naming is treating Environment as decor or as a preference for pretty places. Ra framed correct environment as nurture for the body, and also as what allows a person "to meet the proper other". It is a condition for the aura to be in the right traffic, not an aesthetic.

## Timing, and What Has to Come First

Environment is not the first move. The four transformations run in order, dietary regimen first, then environment, then perspective, then awareness, and none of them replaces Type, Strategy and Authority, which are the catalyst that makes the rest available. Ra placed the arrival of Environment at the Saturn return: "It's between 30 and 50 that you establish through the environment its partner perspective and in that develop the foundation for awareness." Before thirty, in his idealised account of development, the requirements are correct treatment, strategy and authority, and dietary regimen.

So the valley experiment is one of placement over time, running alongside [[Deconditioning|deconditioning]] rather than instead of it. Ra put the stakes plainly: taking correct nutrition into the body does no good if the body is then thrown into an environment and an aura that are wrong for it.
