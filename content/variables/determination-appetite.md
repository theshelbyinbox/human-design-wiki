# Determination: Appetite

Appetite is the first of the six determinations, the dietary regimens read from the colour of the Design Sun and Earth. Its instruction is a single one, and it is severe. Ra Uru Hu, introducing the first colour in his Nutrition lecture, put it this way: "These are people that are designed to eat one thing at a time."

Because any construct in Human Design is read from the bottom up, Appetite is the foundation of the digestive sequence and also the oldest system in it. Ra attached the nickname of the caveman and cavewoman diet to the first colour and described the mechanism behind it plainly: "It is a very, very old digestive system. It isn't able to handle a lot of complexity." Its limitation is not a deficiency but a specification. What is correct for this body is a stream of single foods rather than a combination, and what the food actually is does not matter to the regimen.

Appetite belongs to the lower half of the six. Ra called colours one, two and three the conditions and colours four, five and six the circumstances, and of the three conditions he named the first the most severe, "because it's very rigid". A condition sits inside the eating itself. A circumstance sits in the setting around it. Appetite is the most tightly specified of the conditions, and therefore the most exposed to a food culture organised around mixing, plating and combining.

## Mechanics

Determination is the colour of the Design Sun and Earth. Appetite is colour one of six, and the sequence runs Appetite, Taste, Thirst, Touch, Sound, Light. Colour sits between tone and line in the substructure, and Ra noted that it is "based on the same numerical construct as tone and line", which is why the colours carry the same one to six architecture and the same split between a lower and an upper three.

Each of the six regimens is doubled. As Ra put it, "there are six specific regimens, but those regimens are dualities", and the binary comes from the tone sitting underneath the colour. Tone is also what turns the Determination arrow left or right, so the two sides of Appetite are the left and right expressions of the same one thing at a time rule. Later teaching names these two sides Consecutive and Alternating.

By resonance, colour one belongs with [[line 1]], the base of the lower trigram, and it harmonises with [[line 4]], the base of the upper one. Ra took that relationship down a level explicitly: "there is a harmonic relationship that exists between the first and the fourth color."

## Consecutive and Alternating

The two sides do not change the rule, only the order in which single foods arrive. The library's Human Design dictionary describes the pair like this: "Left Tone Appetite is sequential, finishing one food before starting the next; Right Tone Appetite alternates, switching between foods bite by bite."

Consecutive is therefore the left, active orientation: one food is taken to completion, then the next begins. Alternating is the right, passive orientation: the foods on the plate are still taken singly, but attention moves between them. Both remain inside the condition. Neither side permits the combined mouthful.

The left and right distinction carries no ranking. The Genetic Matrix reference states it directly, that these are not value judgments, and Ra said the same of the six determinations while discussing the move from conditions to circumstances: "Not better. There's no better in any of this."

## What the Name Does Not Mean

The word appetite invites two readings that the recorded material does not support. The first is quantity. Ra rejected volume outright as the measure of correctness: "To be correct isn't about volume. It isn't about how much food you eat or you don't eat." The second is hunger. Nothing in Ra's Nutrition lecture or in the library's dictionary entry defines Appetite as a rule about eating only when genuinely hungry, and the article does not claim it. In this system Appetite is a name for the first colour's regimen, and that regimen is about the singularity of what enters the body, not about how much of it or how urgently it is wanted.

## A Contemporary Framing That Differs

A contemporary Variables guide in the library gives Appetite a different account. It describes the first determination as indicating "your ideal eating pattern and food quantity" and defines the pair as a choice between "larger, structured meals (Consecutive)" and "frequent small meals throughout the day (Alternating)", adding that a simpler diet with minimal food combining suits it.

Only the last part of that overlaps with Ra. The older and more specific account keeps Appetite inside the one thing at a time condition and uses the left and right pair for the order of eating, sequential against switching, not for meal size or meal frequency. The contemporary reading turns the same pair into a statement about portions and timing, which is the thing Ra explicitly set aside. Both readings are in the library. They are presented here as differing, with Ra's as the source account.

## Under Conditioning

Ra treated determination as a genetic predisposition to differentiation and its failure as transference, a predisposition to form homogenization instead. At the physical level the mechanism is simple: "when you're not eating correctly, in other words, when you're not fulfilling what is the demands of your particular digestive system, you're homogenizing your system." The system most affected, in his account, is the brain.

For a first colour the pressure is constant, because almost everything about prepared food runs against the condition. Ra's own illustration was the omelette, a dish with this in it and that in it, put in front of a person whose system can process only one thing at a time. He also named the wider difficulty: "food is one of those things that binds us to others. It can be very alienating." Eating one thing at a time is socially conspicuous in a way that most of the other regimens are not.

## The Harmonic Partner

Following the colour harmonic, the transference partner of Appetite is colour four, [[Determination: Touch|Touch]]. Ra states the general harmonic of first to fourth colour and describes transference as a failure of capture rather than a fault in the colour itself, but he does not walk the six determinations through their transference pairs one by one, so the pairing here follows the harmonic rather than a quoted teaching.

The contrast is instructive even so. Touch is the first of the circumstances and is not about the food at all but about the setting, calm on one side and nervous on the other. A first colour drifting to its harmonic would be a body with a strict internal condition behaving as though the answer lay in the room instead of on the plate.
