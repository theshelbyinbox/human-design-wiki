# Environment

Environment is the second of the four transformations and the bottom left of the four arrows above the head of the bodygraph. It belongs to the body side of Variable and deals with one question: what kind of place a particular body is built to live in. Ra Uru Hu gave the theme a phrase that has stuck to it ever since. "Where you are is who you are."

The claim underneath that phrase is not poetic. It is that the outside nourishes the form as literally as food nourishes it from the inside. Ra called the first transformation the internal and the second the external, the same operation in two directions. "In the same way that it's necessary to differentiate our system within, it's necessary to differentiate our system without out there in the world." Everyone, in this account, is born with a predisposition to align to one correct environment, and that environment is not a preference. It is a health matter, a question of longevity and of what a body can recover from.

The category has six values, running in the usual one to six order: Caves, Markets, Kitchens, Mountains, Valleys and Shores. The names are the most misleading thing about the subject, and Ra spent much of the lecture warning against reading them literally. "It's not about place any more than dietary regimen is about food." Elsewhere in the same talk: "It is your environment that nurtures you. It's the environment, not the place." The six names describe the quality and the structure of a setting, not a location on a map, and "there can be many many many different kinds of environments in relatively the same location."

## Mechanics

Environment is derived from the colour of the Design Nodes. Ra placed it there explicitly: "in the design nodes, what we have is something called environment." The nodes are the axis he associated with the trajectory of a physical life, so this arrow is read as the grounding of that trajectory rather than as a decorative extra.

The colour number, one through six, gives which of the six environments a person carries. The direction the arrow points comes from the tone beneath that colour. Ra described the division of labour between the two layers directly: "color establishes what the standard is and tone creates the nuance of that standard bringing it either to a this or a that." He called the resulting pairs "binary keynotes" and said each environment has one. The arrow's leftness or rightness is therefore not a separate fact bolted onto the environment; it is the environment's own second half.

Ra's own gloss on what the direction shows for this arrow is brief. "We get to see the way in which we involve ourselves in the environment, the way in which we move through our environment. The leftness and the rightness determines, in a larger sense, the framework through which our awareness is going to operate at its optimum." He attached no fixed orientation names to this arrow in the recorded material collected here. Contemporary teaching does, labelling the left-facing arrow Observed and the right-facing arrow Observer, the Observer described as here to watch a setting from outside the action rather than be involved in it. Those two labels belong to later teaching, not to Ra. The general rule for left and right still applies either way, and contemporary reference material puts it plainly: "These are not value judgments." Left is not an upgrade on right.

Ra referred to the nodal arrows as "the independent variable," and contemporary course material builds on that by calling the two bottom arrows the independent variables and the two top arrows dependent, on the reasoning that the upper pair rests on the lower pair being in place first.

## Hardscape and Landscape

Ra split the six into two sets of three. "The lower three are our hardscape and the upper three are called landscape." This mirrors the split he made in [[Determination]], where the lower three colours are conditions and the upper three circumstances.

The hardscape environments are specific. Ra said so flatly: "the lower colors are very very specific." A cave, a market and a kitchen are bounded, built, definable settings, and he credited them with making the human world. "It's the hardscape that created the world," he said, and "the basic construct of our modern world is all there in the first three environments." His image for a city is a stack of the first two: "our cities are nothing but caves piled on top of each other," supported by markets.

The landscape environments are broader. The upper colours, Ra said, are "much more, how can I put this, generalized," not the closed-in specific cave or market or kitchen "but it is a much broader environmental phenomena." Mountains, Valleys and Shores describe the shape of a terrain rather than the walls of a room.

## The Six Environments

### Caves

Caves is the first environment and, like every first line in the system, it is about foundation and security. Ra built the keynote out of one structural fact: a cave has a single obvious entrance. "That allows you to control the doorway. What does that do for you? Well, it provides you with security." A room with one door, an office with one door. His example of the modern cave is the automobile, which a person closes, locks and moves inside. He tied it to health rather than comfort: "If you get sick, go into a room with one door." Contemporary course material splits Caves into Selective and Blending, the blending version favouring a space with more than one way in and more throughflow of others. That pair is later teaching and does not appear in Ra's recorded material here. See [[Environment: Caves]].

### Markets

Markets is the environment of exchange and visible activity. "There are beings that thrive in the marketplace. In other words, there are beings that thrive where there is a high level of activity, where there are things going on, where there is productivity." Ra's recurring picture is the shop with the family living behind it, the open air market, the trading floor, and he noted that the internet has extended the same quality beyond physical markets. Market people, in his description, "can rarely separate themselves from the material process that they're involved with." Contemporary material splits Markets into Internal and External, the internal a space a person creates and invites others into, the external the middle of a bustling community. Those names come only from later course material. See [[Environment: Markets]].

### Kitchens

Kitchens is the environment of production, and Ra was blunt about how far from a domestic kitchen the keynote reaches. "Kitchens are factories. Kitchens are any place where there is production going on, manufacturing going on." A workshop, a laboratory, a studio. It sits next to Markets and is easily confused with it; the distinction drawn in the sources is that Markets is about exchange and Kitchens about something being made or changed. Contemporary teaching splits Kitchens into Wet and Dry, described as humid versus dry or desert climates. That pair is later teaching only. See [[Environment: Kitchens]].

### Mountains

Mountains opens the landscape half and is about height. "Mountain is about oxygen actually," Ra said. "You know the higher you go the less oxygen you take in." These are people who need to get to the high ground, in his phrase, and the requirement scales: it does not mean Everest, it can mean the upper floor of a two storey house, or the high point of a flat plain. Both of Ra's sons are Mountains, and he described putting them upstairs rather than on the ground floor, and keeping them upstairs when ill. Mountains carries a vantage point as well as an altitude, the position of looking down over the whole. The split into Active and Passive Mountains is supported in the contemporary material in this library, not in Ra's own. See [[Environment: Mountains]].

### Valleys

Valleys is low ground understood as a corridor. "Valleys are places where information flows. It is through valleys that all transportation moves, activity." These are people who need movement and activity around them while they sit below it and watch, and Ra's image is the person on the bench on a busy street watching everyone go past. He added a social consequence: valley people tend to meet the unusual outsider, because they sit where flows of people and information cross, and so interact outside whatever their normal grouping would be. Contemporary material adds an acoustic reading, sound carrying and dispersing through open surroundings, and splits Valleys into Narrow and Wide. See [[Environment: Valleys]].

### Shores

Shores is the sixth and the one Ra spoke about most personally, because it is his own. A shore is an edge, the line where one environment becomes another. His binary for it is the only environment binary he names in this library: "I am a shore person and I'm here to be on natural shores as opposed to unnatural shores or artificial shores." A natural shore is water meeting land. Contemporary teaching extends the artificial version to any constructed transition, the edge of a town, a border, a street dividing two neighbourhoods. Ra's account of his own life is the strongest health claim anywhere in the material: born on an island in the middle of a river, living beside water for all but two years, and in those two years "I was the sickest I have ever been and my partner who is also shores nearly died and we nearly lost our second child." He and his partner met on the shoreline, in the environment correct for both of them. See [[Environment: Shores]].

## Environment and Perspective

Environment's partner is [[Perspective]], derived from the colour of the Personality Nodes, and Ra treated the two as one nodal mechanism seen from body side and mind side. "If you're in the correct environment, then you're going to see what you're here to see. Because what you find on the personality side in the personality nodes is your perspective." Put the other way round: "You cannot take advantage of the unique perspective if you're not in the right place looking at the right things."

He was specific about when this becomes a live issue. An idealised upbringing asks only two things of a child before roughly thirty: that it is raised on its own [[Strategy]] and [[Inner Authority]], and that it follows its dietary regimen. Environment arrives at the Saturn return. "It's at 30 at the Saturn return. This is when the idealized upbringing takes on the demand of environment. It's between 30 and 50 that you establish through the environment its partner perspective and in that develop the foundation for awareness." That twenty year window is the middle stretch of the proper development of a nine centred being, and it is why he told parents not to feel guilty about children who are not in their own correct environment.

The nodes are also where the body meets other people. "It's on the nodal line that you meet the other," Ra said, which makes environment a matter of association as well as health. Being in the correct environment, in his description, "eliminates your resistance in terms of the way you're going to connect to the other being."

## Where Environment Sits in the Chain

The four transformations run in a fixed order: dietary regimen, then environment, then perspective, then conceptualising and awareness. Environment is second, downstream of [[Determination]] and upstream of everything mental. Ra's statement of the dependency is unusually forceful: "If the mind does not have access to correct perspective, then the mind can never, ever, ever, ever, ever be correct. And you cannot have the correct perspective if you're not in the right place. And if you're not in the right place, and you're not eating correctly, then there is no way that you're going to go through any of these transformations."

None of this replaces the surface of the chart. Variable is entered after Strategy and Authority are working, not instead of them. Ra allowed one practical concession, that a conditioned adult can begin "with your dietary regimen and your environmental distinction at the same time."

## How the Names Are Meant to Be Read

The single most common error with this arrow is literalism. Ra: "I'm not talking about locations. I'm not talking about this city or that city or this country or that country." Contemporary teaching says the same in plainer language, that seeing the word Mountains on a chart does not mean a person has to move to a mountain.

What the material asks for instead is deconstruction, which Ra described as a skill that has to be developed: "One of the things you have to develop in environments is a whole new way of deconstructing these keys." The automobile as a cave is his own worked example, and the kitchen that is really a factory is another. Each of the six turns up in settings that do not look like the word, and a person who cannot move can often build the quality into where they already live. Ra's own examples are domestic and small: which floor a child sleeps on, which room someone recovers in.

The names also conceal that environment is presented as a health mechanism rather than a mood. Ra: "More than anything else, environment is about quality of life."

## The State of the Material

Environment is the thinnest of the four Variable categories in this library. Ra's "Orientation" lecture is effectively the whole primary source, and it is a single overview talk that gives each of the six a few sentences rather than a treatment. There is no recorded lecture here that works through the six in depth, no per environment breakdown from Ra of the kind that exists for the digestions, and the Holistic Analysis material that would cover environment diagnostics appears here only as a table of contents.

Three gaps are worth naming. First, the binaries. Ra names only one of the six pairs, natural and artificial Shores. Mountains Active and Passive and Valleys Narrow and Wide come from contemporary teaching, and Caves Selective and Blending, Markets Internal and External and Kitchens Wet and Dry from contemporary course material alone. One contemporary source also refers in passing to "kitchens hot or kitchens wet," which does not match the Wet and Dry pair given elsewhere, so even the later material is not settled.

Second, the orientation labels. Observed and Observer are in wide contemporary use for this arrow and are not in Ra's recorded material here.

Third, Ra's own account of which substructure layer produces the binary is not consistent. In the Orientation lecture he assigns the standard to colour and the nuance to tone. In his colour lecture he says of his own natural shores that "it is the color that determines what value in that environment, whether it's going to be natural or whether it's going to be artificial." The first is the statement made while teaching the arrows directly and matches how the rest of Variable is described, so it is the one followed here, but the second is noted rather than smoothed over.
