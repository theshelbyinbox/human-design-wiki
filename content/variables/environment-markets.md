# Environment: Markets

Markets is the second of the six Environments, and the second of the three that Ra Uru Hu grouped together as hardscape. It names a setting defined by exchange and traffic: a place where goods, money, ideas and people pass through each other rather than settle. Ra introduced it in the Orientation lecture as the step that follows Caves in what he framed as an evolutionary sequence, calling the movement from cave to market "a natural progression."

The word is a structural description, not a location. Ra was emphatic about this when he opened the Environment material: "I'm not talking about locations. I'm not talking about this city or that city or this country or that country." A market is not a farmers market or a stock exchange in particular. It is any setting carrying the quality of commerce and throughflow, and the analytic work, as Ra put it, is a matter of "deconstructing these keys" so that the ancient word can be read into a modern life.

Environment is the second of the four transformations, and the corpus places it squarely in the body rather than the mind. It is about well-being on the outside, in the same way that [[PHS & Diet|dietary regimen]] is about well-being on the inside. Ra's summary of the whole theme was "where you are is who you are."

## Mechanics

The Environment arrow is the bottom left arrow of the four, and it derives from the colour of the Design Nodes. Markets is colour 2. Since the Design and Personality Nodes are the axis Ra called the independent variable, Environment sits among the foundational arrows, the ones the two upper arrows depend on. In the spine's terms, the Design Nodes "represent the second of transformations," and "when you're looking at the design nodes, you're looking at your well-being in relationship to the outside."

Colour establishes which of the six environments applies. Tone, the layer beneath colour, supplies the nuance and decides which way the arrow points. Ra described the relationship plainly: colour "establishes what the standard is and tone creates the nuance of that standard bringing it either to a this or a that." He referred to the resulting pairs as binary keynotes attached to each environment, but in this library he does not name the two Markets keynotes.

Contemporary course material does name them, as Markets Internal and Markets External. Internal is described there as a space you have created for business and invite others into, energised by exchange as long as you keep control of where the trading happens, and shared preferentially with people who hold a similar interest or goal. External is described as being in the middle of the action in a community, favouring bustling settings with dynamic energy, with specific goals in mind and real care about who is engaged. These labels should be read as later teaching rather than as Ra's own, and the same caution applies to the Observed and Observer labels sometimes attached to the direction of the Environment arrow generally.

## A Place of Exchange

Ra's description of Markets is about density of activity and the material life that runs through it. "There are beings that thrive in the marketplace," he said. "In other words, there are beings that thrive where there is a high level of activity, where there are things going on, where there is productivity."

His examples are deliberately ordinary and deliberately old. The open air market, "literally the marketplace literally where everything was done, where all the business, all the trade." The shop that has existed since the first cities, where "you sell the goods in the front" and the family sleeps in the back. And then the modern extension of the same quality: "the new marketplace is something that is making it possible for beings to be in the market anywhere, the internet." What matters is not the era or the architecture but the presence of trade and movement.

One consequence Ra drew from this is temperamental as well as environmental. Of Markets people he said, "these are people that can rarely separate themselves from the material process that they're involved with." The environment that nourishes them is the one their livelihood runs through.

Contemporary teaching fills in the picture with the town square, the mall, the co-working space, the trading floor, the studio people can walk into and out of, and describes the keynotes as exchange, networking and the showcasing of talent. The recurring images are throughflow and transience: people come, people go, and exchanges of money or ideas happen in the passing.

## The Hardscape Grouping

Ra divided the six environments in half. "The lower three are our hardscape and the upper three are called landscape." The hardscape environments, Caves, Markets and Kitchens, are the specific and enclosed ones; the landscape environments are, in his phrasing, "a much broader environmental phenomena." This mirrors the division in Determination, where the lower three colours are conditions and the upper three are circumstances.

Ra gave the hardscape a civilizational weight. "It's the hardscape that created the world," he said, and Markets sits at the centre of that claim. "This is what one two and three these colors, they built our cities." His image of the city is worth keeping: "Our cities are nothing but caves piled on top of each other," and "our cities are nothing but caves that are supported by the markets." Caves are where people shelter. Markets are what keeps them fed.

## Timing and the Partner Arrow

Environment is not an infant concern. Ra held that until the Saturn return at roughly thirty, what a child needs is to be treated correctly, to operate by its own strategy and authority, and to follow its dietary regimen. "It's at 30 at the Saturn return. This is when the idealized upbringing takes on the demand of environment." What follows is the window in which Markets, or any environment, does its real work: "It's between 30 and 50 that you establish through the environment its partner perspective and in that develop the foundation for awareness."

That partnership is mechanical, not poetic. Environment comes from the Design Nodes and [[Perspective]] from the Personality Nodes, and Ra treated the two as linked by nature. "You cannot take advantage of the unique perspective if you're not in the right place looking at the right things." Being in the wrong environment is therefore not only uncomfortable; it blocks the transformation above it in the chain. None of this substitutes for [[Strategy|strategy]] and authority, which remain the catalyst.

## How It Is Commonly Misread

The most frequent error is literalism. Markets does not mean a person belongs in retail, in sales, or in a physical bazaar, any more than Caves means living underground.

The second error is confusing Markets with [[Environment: Kitchens|Kitchens]]. Contemporary teaching acknowledges the two are routinely mixed up, and draws the line at what the setting is for: Markets is commerce and exchange, Kitchens is production, a place where something is being made or transformed. Ra's own gloss on Kitchens, that "kitchens are factories," points the same way.

The third is treating environment as preference. In the corpus it is consistently a health matter. Ra's advice for a sick child with this environment was not to isolate them but to do the opposite of what instinct suggests: take them to work, "keep them around the buzz."
