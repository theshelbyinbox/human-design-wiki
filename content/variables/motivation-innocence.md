# Motivation: Innocence

Innocence is the sixth colour of Motivation, the layer of substructure beneath the Personality Sun and Earth. It is the strangest of the six, because Ra Uru Hu describes it as a motivation that is not a motivation at all. Where the other five colours name something that drives a person from underneath, the sixth names the absence of that drive.

Motivation is one of the four Variable arrows and it belongs to the mind side of the chart. Ra distinguishes the two faces of colour plainly: "When we look at color on the personality side it's called motivation. Motivation it is the underlying background frequency that that drives the surface." Colour, in his framing, is the answer to the actor's question about the script. The line is the role. The colour is what the performance comes from.

This article is about the sixth colour of Motivation, not about [[Gate 25]], whose keynote in Ra's own book is "Innocence, the spirit of the self". The two share a word and nothing else.

## Mechanics

Motivation is read from the colour of the Personality Sun and Earth. The six values in order are Fear, Hope, Desire, Need, Guilt and Innocence. Some of those names come from later teaching rather than from Ra's recorded lectures, and [[Motivation]] sets out which is which.

Colour gives the arrow its category, not its direction. The Motivation arrow points left for strategic and right for receptive, and it is tone, the layer below colour, that decides which. Colour six tells you the theme and nothing about left or right.

Ra grounds the category in the ordinary sense of the word motive: "the motive for everything we do is in the color." He is equally plain that it is not available to introspection: "There is an underlying motivation we have no access to affecting how we end up living things out on the surface."

## No Motivation, Anti Motivation

The core statement comes from Ra's substructure lecture on colour, where he sets the sixth against the third:

"The third color motivation is the motivation of desire ... On the sixth, you have something that's totally different. You have something that is innocent. It is totally without motivation. Think about it. The third color full of desire is a motivation. The desire to lead. Six color. No motivation. No motivation. Anti motivation."

The paradox is the point. Every other colour supplies a reason underneath the role. Fear drives the first line's search for a solid foundation, desire drives the third towards leading. The sixth supplies nothing of that kind. The role is simply lived, with no agenda beneath it pushing it somewhere.

Ra immediately adds that this is theory rather than observation: "Now, this is the way they're supposed to be naturally, but this is not the way it works in the world." He then points at what the world actually contains: "Think about all those people who are motivated in the notself world to be leaders. Now, these are all beings who actually have a six color and should have no motivation at all. None. None. Certainly not to be a leader." The ambition to lead is, in his reading, the clearest evidence that a six colour is not running as a six colour.

## Colour Six and the Sixth Line

Ra describes colour, tone and line as three sets of six stacked on each other, with colour in the middle, and each six divided into a lower three and an upper three the way a hexagram divides into two trigrams. That division creates fixed pairings: "The first line and the fourth line have what is called a harmonic relationship. The two and the five, the three and the six." The same structure repeats one level down, between "the third and the sixth" colour.

Between colour and line the relationship works by number. "If you have a first line and you have a first color, they're in resonance ... If you have a first color and you have a fourth line, that's a harmony ... But if it's dissonance ... It is a process that in fact is much more uncomfortable." Applied to the sixth colour, Innocence resonates with a sixth line and harmonises with a third.

Contemporary teaching reads that resonance the same way, tying the sixth motivation to the sixth line's view of the whole from above, impersonal rather than concerned with what one wants from it.

## Every Transitional Profile Carries It

The most concrete thing said about who carries this colour concerns the transitional profiles. Ra states it in the 1/4 lecture: "all transitional profiles, all of them have a six color personality. It doesn't matter whether you're a 1,4 or a 6,3, or a 3,6 or a 4,1 or a 2,5 or a 5,2, you're all going to have a six color personality, which means that as transitional harmonic profiles, you also all have as a basic design, the same motivation."

He returns to it in the 2/5 lecture, naming the colour outright: "That motivation is something that is referred to as innocence. And one of the most important things about transitional profiles is their struggle with in their life in dealing with a homogenized world to try to maintain the innocence of their role in this life. Most of them do not."

In the 5/2 lecture the same point returns as a lament about conditioned children: "the six-color motivation under all transitional profiles for the personality. Innocence. Innocence. You know, and not its transference, desire, this is what we get."

## Transference to Desire

Transference is the harmonic slippage that happens under conditioning, and for Innocence the slippage is to [[Motivation: Desire]] on the six to three harmonic. Ra calls it "the byproduct of incorrectness" and describes the mechanism as a failure of capture rather than a fault in the colour: "Let's say that that color frequency happens to be a six. It is emitted perfectly ... there's nothing wrong with the color. The problem is always in the cap ... So instead of getting a six, it pulls in a three. And that three is not substantiated by the tonal values underneath it." Elsewhere he puts it more bluntly: "It just can't carry the six."

That is why the expected distortion of Innocence is Desire, and why it shows up as wanting to lead. Ra's own case runs the other way. He describes himself as a third colour Personality Sun and Earth who in transference to the sixth "didn't want to take responsibility for anything I didn't want to lead anything", and calls it "a very easy way out".

In the 1/4 lecture he widens the point. Because the transitional profiles bridge the other profiles, and all carry the sixth colour, the transference distorts what those bridges carry: "rather than a world that has as its bridge, innocence as its motivation, desire is usually what takes place."

## Names That Collide

Two further cautions beyond [[Gate 25]]. Ra introduces the fifth colour with the words "Here in our binary we have guilt and innocence", so the word also appears as the counterpole of [[Motivation: Guilt]]. He then defines that binary as either accepting conditioning or being the conditioner, which is a separate matter from the sixth colour.

Then the binary itself. Ra gives explicit poles for some Motivation colours, communalist and separatist for Fear, follower and leader for Desire, conditioner and accepting conditioning for Guilt. For the sixth he gives none, and any pair offered elsewhere should be treated as an addition rather than as Ra's.

## Strategy and Authority Come First

Nothing in the Variable layer is reachable by deciding to reach it. Ra ties access to the substructure to the decision making authority of the body: "The moment you take authority away from the mind is the moment that you align yourself differently ... you immediately begin to align your frequency so that the monopole is going to capture the right color."

So [[Strategy]] and [[Inner Authority]] are the precondition, not an optional first step, and the timescale is long. Ra puts cellular deconditioning at seven years. His summary in the 4/1 lecture is the plainest statement of what is at stake here: "You don't have to lose your innocence, but you have to find your truth."
