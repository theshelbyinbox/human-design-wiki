# Environment: Kitchens

Kitchens is the third of the six environments, and the last of the three that Ra Uru Hu grouped as the hardscape. It describes a setting in which something is being made. Not bought, not sold, not withdrawn into, but produced: raw material going in one state and coming out another. The name is a figure for a structural quality of a place, and Ra was blunt that it should not be read literally. "Kitchens are factories," he said. "Kitchens are any place where there is production going on, manufacturing going on."

Environment is the second of the four transformations, and it is the arrow that answers the question of where the body belongs rather than who it belongs with. Ra opened his lecture on the subject with the line "where you are is who you are," and returned repeatedly to the point that the correct environment is not a location. "It is your environment that nurtures you. It's the environment, not the place. It's not about place. There can be many many many different kinds of environments in relatively the same location." A kitchens person is not looking for a city or a country. They are looking for the presence of production, wherever it happens to sit on a map.

Of the six environments, this is the one Ra noticed people liked the sound of and understood the least. "All my students love the third one, kitchens. seems so cute. But you know kitchens are many things."

## Mechanics

Environment derives from the COLOR of the Design Nodes, and Kitchens is colour 3 of that arrow. It sits in the bottom left position of the four arrows, on the body side of the design, and it is paired across the bottom of the chart with [[Perspective]], which comes from the Personality Nodes. Ra treated that pairing as structural rather than incidental: environment "is nodal and because it's going to connect in that sense to the personality node which is the theme of perspective."

Colour sets the category and tone decides the direction. Ra described the relationship plainly: "color establishes what the standard is and tone creates the nuance of that standard bringing it either to a this or a that in that case." He confirmed that each environment carries what he called "binary keynotes," a left and a right expression, but the recording in this library does not name the pair for Kitchens. What is named for Kitchens comes from later sources, and those sources do not agree with each other. See below.

## What a Kitchen Actually Is

The defining feature is transformation of material, and Ra's own extension of the image was industrial: factories, manufacturing, anywhere production is happening. Contemporary teaching extends it the same direction. One guide summarises Kitchens as "Transformation, creativity, and production," and describes people with this environment as excelling "in productive, hands-on settings where things are being made, altered, or improved." A contemporary podcast treatment adds working examples: a shop, a lab, a studio, a work room where a team is building something together. "A laboratory would be a kitchen."

That same treatment draws the line between Kitchens and its neighbour [[Environment: Markets|Markets]], confused with each other because both are busy and both involve other people. The distinction is what the busyness is for. "It is about the production of something. Not the commerce, but something's being created there." A market is exchange. A kitchen is manufacture.

Ra also treated environment as a health matter rather than a preference, and gave a concrete instance for this colour. If a kitchens child is ill, "bring them into the kitchen. No joke. I mean, you just bring them into the kitchen, you know, in the same way that you would put them in their cave, in the same way that you would take them to work if you were a market." The correct environment is where the body recovers.

## The Third Environment and the Hardscape

The six environments split three and three. "The lower three are our hardscape and the upper three are called landscape," Ra said, which makes Kitchens the closing term of the hardscape set alongside [[Environment: Caves|Caves]] and Markets. He gave that grouping real weight. "It's the hardscape that created the world. You know, this is really what it did. You know, our modern world, everything about the nature of our modern world, the basic construct of our modern world is all there in the first three environments."

The split also carries a difference in resolution. Ra drew the parallel to [[Determination]], where the lower colours are conditions and the upper colours are circumstances, and said the same gradient applies here: the lower environments are "very very specific," while the upper three are "much more how can I put this generalized." Kitchens is therefore the most generalised of the specific environments and the last before the scale widens out to [[Environment: Mountains|Mountains]], [[Environment: Valleys|Valleys]] and [[Environment: Shores|Shores]]. It is still a built, bounded, human place.

## Left and Right, and a Disagreement in the Sources

Ra established that a binary exists for every environment, but this library does not record his keynotes for Kitchens. Two contemporary sources fill the gap, and they fill it differently.

A contemporary course deck gives the pair as Wet and Dry, and reads both as climate. Its shared definition calls a kitchen "an overarching term for a creative space in which transformation happens. A space where the community gathers to create/transform themselves and others," with the collective connecting and changing things "from one state to another - transmutation." On that reading, Wet Kitchens "specifically indicate a humid climate which favors high humidity," and Dry Kitchens "specifically indicate a drier, desert-like or high mountain climate with low humidity."

A contemporary podcast, describing what a reader will see printed on a chart, says in passing that it may read "kitchens hot or kitchens wet." That names a different axis, temperature rather than humidity, and it is stated in passing rather than taught.

Both are contemporary and neither can be traced to Ra in this library. They are reported as they stand, because nothing in the source material settles which is right.

## How Kitchens Is Commonly Misread

The literal reading is the main one. "We say kitchen and people are like, I need to be in the kitchen." Nothing requires a kitchens person to cook, although there is no reason they cannot. The second misreading is treating it as a synonym for busy. Kitchens is not activity in general; it is activity that produces. The third is treating it as a personality trait, when environment is a bodily alignment rather than a description of character.

Ra's timing note is also routinely skipped. Environment becomes a requirement at the Saturn return, not before. "It's between 30 and 50 that you establish through the environment its partner perspective and in that develop the foundation for awareness." He told parents specifically not to feel guilty about a child not being in its correct environment.
