# The Four Arrows

Four small arrows sit at the top of a Human Design chart, two above the design column and two above the personality column. Each one points either left or right. Taken together they are what Ra Uru Hu called Variable, and they describe how a body is built to take in nourishment, where it is built to be, how a mind is built to look, and what drives that mind when it conceptualizes.

Variable arrived late in the development of the knowledge and sits at the far end of the [[Variables Overview|Variables material]]. Ra dated his own detailed work on the substructure to the years of the primary health system and rave psychology programs, and he dedicated his 2008 teaching year to Variable specifically. It is not a separate system bolted onto the bodygraph. It is what the layers already beneath every gate and line look like when four particular positions are read together.

The arrows are also not a personality inventory. Ra was consistent that what they point to is cognitive architecture, the plan for how a self-reflected consciousness is designed to operate. As he put it in Footnotes to Variable, "The variable is rooted in what is the pure essence of our purpose, that is the cognitive potential that is there at the tonal level that determines the way in which those arrows are going to point one way or another."

## Where the Arrows Come From

Every activation in a chart is usually read down to two levels, the gate and the line. Beneath the line the structure keeps going. Beneath each line are six colours. Beneath each colour are six tones. Beneath each tone is base. Ra described the whole stack as built upward rather than downward: "it is the tone that leads to the color that leads to the line that leads to the gate and so forth and so on." The scale of it is easy to underestimate. Of the substructure under a single line he said, "There is over a thousand variations for each line."

Variable reads that substructure at four positions only: the design Sun and Earth, the design Nodes, the personality Nodes, and the personality Sun and Earth. Colour at those four positions gives the four arrow categories. Tone at those four positions gives [[Cognition]] and also decides which way each arrow points.

Colour itself is named differently depending on which side of the chart it falls. Ra was explicit about this. On the design side colour is called determination and it is rooted in digestion. On the personality side colour is called motivation, the underlying drive beneath the role a person is here to play. He liked the theatrical version of it, the actor holding the script and asking the director what the motivation for the line is.

Tone is the layer that makes the arrows point. "It's tone that establishes whether something is to the left or something is to the right," Ra said in his lecture on tone. Tone is drawn as a triangle divided down the middle, six tones arranged in three binaries: the splenic binary at the lower left with tones one and two, the ajna binary at the apex with tones three and four, and the solar plexus binary at the lower right with tones five and six. The dividing line runs through the ajna binary, which splits tones one, two and three to the left and tones four, five and six to the right. This is why cognition is not a fifth arrow. It is the layer underneath all four of them.

Base sits below tone and is not a Variable category at all. Ra described base as the entry frequency, the moment a neutrino strikes the crystal, and its practical theme as orientation rather than direction. It is drawn as a square, tone as a triangle, colour as a six-sided figure.

## The Four Positions

Each arrow occupies a fixed corner, and each corner has a fixed derivation and a fixed pair of orientation names.

The top left arrow is [[Determination]], derived from the design Sun and Earth. Its orientations are active to the left and passive to the right. Ra usually called this arrow the brain, and he described the leftness and rightness here as "the activity level of the brain, whether it is active or passive." This is the arrow that yields dietary regimen, the conditions and circumstances under which a body should take nutrition in.

The bottom left arrow is [[Environment]], derived from the design Nodes. Ra put the emphasis on the nodes as the physical trajectory of a life: "it is the nodes that guide us along our trajectory in this life. And the grounding of this trajectory is what we find in the design nodes." Environment is not a place on a map. It is the kind of setting in which a body operates well. Contemporary reference material labels this arrow's two directions observed and observer, but those labels do not appear in Ra's recorded material held in this library.

The bottom right arrow is [[Perspective]], derived from the personality Nodes. Its orientations are focused to the left and peripheral to the right. Perspective is what becomes available once the body is in the correct environment. Ra: "if you're in the correct environment, then you're going to see what you're here to see."

The top right arrow is [[Motivation]], derived from the personality Sun and Earth. Its orientations are strategic to the left and receptive to the right. Ra usually called this arrow the mind, and he treated it as the final step, "all about conceptualizing," the place where awareness and outer authority become possible.

Ra also grouped the two nodal arrows together and called them the storyline, since both are rooted in the nodes and both concern direction and place rather than brain and mind.

## What Left and Right Mean

Left and right are not decoration on the categories. They are the mutation at the centre of the whole system. Ra tied leftness to the seven-centred being that preceded us and rightness to the mutation in the visual cortex that he dated to 1781. The nine-centred form carries both. He called it an interregnum, a transitional form, homo sapiens in transitus.

His own compact statement of the six terms is worth quoting whole, because the pairs are not interchangeable across arrows: "the difference between being strategic, which is left, and being receptive, which is right, being active, which is left, being passive, which is right, being focused, which is left, being peripheral, which is right."

The left mind is focused and strategic, and in its focus, Ra said, it loses its periphery. The right mind is what he called the new mind, a mutation in how the visual cortex assembles information. Where the left mind focuses and draws a line to memory, the right takes in broadly and deeply. He described it as a well and a deep creative resource, something drawn out by others rather than aimed by its owner. The left brain is active and busy. The right brain is passive and absorbent, not intended to be driven to high activity.

None of this is a ranking. Contemporary reference material states it plainly: "These are not value judgments." Ra said the same thing in his own idiom when discussing the intersection variables, that none of the sixteen is more difficult, less difficult, better or worse than another. The same material also warns against the most common conflation, which is mistaking Variable's left and right for the pop-psychology split between an analytical left hemisphere and a creative right one. Variable describes modes of orientation within the system, not two personality styles.

## Body Side and Mind Side

The four arrows fall into two pairs, and reading them as pairs is what makes a configuration legible.

The design side arrows, [[Determination]] and [[Environment]], are the body side. They describe how the body takes in and processes, and how it integrates with its surroundings. The personality side arrows, [[Perspective]] and [[Motivation]], are the mind side. They describe how the mind views and how the mind thinks.

This maps directly onto what Ra called binary consciousness. "We're a binary consciousness, it's something truly to grasp about us," he said, describing the long human error of asking the personality to both run the life and explore it intellectually. Strategy and authority hand the running of the life back to the form. Variable then describes what each half of the binary is built to do once that handover has been made.

## The Sixteen Configurations

Four arrows, each with two possible directions, produce sixteen configurations. Ra was emphatic that these are groupings rather than labels: "There are 16 possibilities. These are the 16 variables." He also called them fractal families, and said that two people sharing a Variable share something deeply profound, "the same general way."

He laid them out as a grid and clearly enjoyed it. "That's a variable graph. For me, it's a magic square." The coding runs design first and personality second, so the upper left box is all left, design left left over personality left left, and the lower right box is all right. Reading the grid from that upper left corner down to that lower right corner traces, in his words, "a mapping of the transition through the variable groupings from the left, where we come from, to the right, where we're going to."

Several structural features of the grid recur in his teaching. The two corners are the extremes. The all left is the only configuration carrying no rightness at all, purely strategic, active and focused. The all right is deeply receptive, with no natural strategic skills whatsoever. Ra found the irony of this instructive, since both extremes depend on strategy and authority more than anyone, the all right most of all.

The four boxes at the centre of the grid he called the intersection, a place of confluence where leftness and rightness collide. He associated the intersection with genuine difficulty in the not-self, including confusion between left and right brain function, and he stressed correct nourishment for children carrying these configurations. He also described a divide running down the middle of the grid, noticing while teaching all sixteen workshops that everything to the left of it carried a distinct leftness regardless of its internal mix, and everything to the right carried a distinct rightness.

He organised those workshops by the grid's four vertical columns, which are the personality columns, on the reasoning that personality "is who we think we are" and is therefore what people reflect most clearly. The horizontal rows are the design. One further structural note he made about the lower left grouping is that no one with a transitional profile can belong to it.

## Transference and Distraction

The arrows describe what is correct. They also describe what happens when a person is not correct, and the mechanic for that is transference.

Ra located it in the relationship between colour and the magnetic monopole. The colour being emitted is always perfect, he insisted; the problem is never the colour, it is the capture. Under a distorted frequency the monopole pulls in the harmonic instead of the true value. The harmonics run one to four, two to five, three to six, exactly as they do between profile lines.

His own statement of it is short: "The one to the four, the two to the five, the three to the six, or the other way around. In a sense, one's motivation is always being pulled away. It's always being pulled away, being pulled into transference by conditioning. This same pulling away with the nodes is called distraction."

So the same mechanic carries two names. At the Sun and Earth, and therefore in [[Motivation]], it is transference. At the nodes, and therefore in [[Perspective]], it is distraction. Ra gave a worked example of transference from his own chart, describing himself as a third colour personality Sun Earth whose true motivation is desire and who, in transference, wanted to lead nothing and take responsibility for nothing. For distraction he gave an equally direct one: "I'm a six. My view, my perspective is personal, but the distraction is a view to power."

Of the perspective pairs, only possibility and probability are named outright by Ra in this library. The other two follow the same one to four, two to five, three to six harmonic, but they are not quoted in the material held here.

## The Chain, and Why the Order Matters

Ra did not treat the four arrows as four independent readings. They are stages, and they run in a fixed order: dietary regimen, then environment, then perspective, then conceptualizing. Each one is the precondition for the next, which is why the arrows are also called [[The Four Transformations]].

His clearest statement of the dependency is a warning rather than a promise. "If the mind does not have access to correct perspective, then the mind can never, ever, ever, ever, ever be correct. And you cannot have the correct perspective if you're not in the right place. And if you're not in the right place, and you're not eating correctly, then there is no way that you're going to go through any of these transformations."

The chain is also slow. Ra described cellular deconditioning as a seven-year process and said flatly that the deeper deconditioning begins with food. He gave a developmental frame for the two nodal arrows in particular, tying them to the Saturn return: "It's between 30 and 50 that you establish through the environment its partner perspective and in that develop the foundation for awareness." Contemporary reference material states the chain in the same sequence, from correct cognition in the brain, to the correct environment, to the view that environment locks in, to a mind that can function as a unique outer authority.

## Why Variable Is Not a Shortcut

Variable does not replace [[types|Type]], [[Strategy]] or [[The Authority Hierarchy|Authority]], and it does not work without them. Ra's framing was that strategy and authority are the catalyst, and that the catalyst has to fire first: "So first the catalyzation and then you enter into the variable itself."

The reason is mechanical rather than moral. Transference and distraction are what a conditioned monopole does. Decisions made from the mental plane keep the frequency distorted, so the correct colour never lands. Strategy and authority change the frequency, which is what allows the arrows to describe anything real about a life instead of describing a not-self version of it. Ra said the same thing from the other direction when discussing the all right configuration, which has the least native strategic capacity and therefore the greatest dependence on strategy and authority: "In the end, all human design comes back to the very root."

He also declined to oversell it. Variable, he said, "guarantees nothing, promises nothing," and awareness is "quite a process to get there."

## Common Misreadings

A handful of errors recur often enough to be worth naming.

Treating left as better than right. It is an orientation, not a rank, and the grid's direction of travel does not make one end superior.

Reading one arrow in isolation. Each arrow can be studied alone, but the meaning emerges from the four-arrow pattern. Ra's own reading of the intersection depended entirely on how the brain and mind arrows sat against the storyline beneath them.

Using Variable as a personality brand. Contemporary reference material is blunt that Variable describes orientation and differentiation, not a slogan or a comparison tool, and that forcing yourself toward a preferred configuration is not-self behaviour. The configuration does not change over a lifetime; only a person's clarity about it does. It does depend on an accurate birth time, since small differences can flip an arrow.

Confusing two uses of one word. Ra called Motivation colour four "wanting," saying "the motivation of the four is what it wants." Later teaching names Motivation four Need and reserves Wanting for [[Perspective: Wanting|Perspective four]]. The same word therefore names two different things depending on the source, and the arrow it belongs to has to be specified. Similarly, the name Hope for Motivation colour two comes from contemporary teaching; Ra distinguished colour one and colour two as exoteric and esoteric.

## A Contested Point About Reading Order

One contemporary reference page states that on the personality side the correct order for Variable correctness is Perspective first and Mind Style second, and adds that the standard display convention "was originally specified incorrectly by Ra Uru Hu and later acknowledged as such."

That is a contemporary claim, and this library holds no corroboration for it. No recorded Ra material in the corpus contains any such acknowledgement. What Ra's own material does support is the sequence of the chain, in which perspective precedes conceptualizing, so the underlying ordering is consistent with his teaching even though the attribution of a correction to him is not evidenced here.
