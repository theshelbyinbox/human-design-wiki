# Inform -- Manifestor Strategy

Inform is the strategy of the [[Manifestor]], and the second half of the [[Respond|Respond and Inform]] strategy of the [[Manifesting Generator]]. Manifestors are the one type that can initiate without an external cue, and informing is what lets that energy move through a world of waiting types without being fought at every turn.

Sources vary on the population figure. Ra Uru Hu's teaching transcripts say about 9 percent, a published aura-types reference document lists 9.7 percent, and The Human Design Dictionary, Jovian Archive, gives roughly 10 percent. The wiki's Types section publishes around 9 percent with an honest range of roughly 8 to 11.5 percent, and this page follows it.

## Why informing works at all

The mechanical reason sits in the aura. The Manifestor aura is closed and repelling: not merely hard to read from outside, it actively pushes the other aura away ([[Manifestor]], [[Aura Types]]). Nobody can see what a Manifestor is about to do, so the action lands on people who had no warning, and what comes back is suspicion, resistance, or an attempt to slow them down.

Informing does not open the aura. It opens one door, once, about one thing. The Human Design Dictionary, Jovian Archive, describes it as a simple act that builds trust and clears the way for the Manifestor to move without resistance. Ra's image for it is a car and a pedestrian.

> "I call it the manifestor driving down the street who sees somebody walking across in front of them. It is not about applying the brakes. That's not what informing is. It's about honking the horn."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

The Manifestor does not stop. It makes a noise so the pedestrian can move. Ra held that the road then clears, and that the Manifestor often pulls many along in its wake.

## What informing is not

Almost every failure to inform comes from mistaking informing for something heavier than it is.

- **Not asking permission.** An adult Manifestor is not requesting authorisation and is not waiting for an answer. Ra: "Informing means you're letting those who need to know know. But not so that they can tell you no. Simply for you to be able to tell them what's going to happen. That's all."
- **Not justification.** What is informed is the coming action, not the reasoning. A Manifestor who explains why has turned a notice into a defence.
- **Not a discussion.** A negotiation invites the other person to argue, which is the resistance the strategy exists to remove.
- **Not an apology.** Informing admits nothing and asks nothing.
- **Not a speech.** In Ra's phrasing it does not have to be profound, articulated communication. It can just be letting the other know.

## Who needs to be informed

The test is impact, not hierarchy. Anyone whose life or plans will change because of what the Manifestor is about to do hears it first, whether or not they have authority over it: partners and family, colleagues before a pivot on shared work, friends before a change of plan, anyone who has arranged their day around the Manifestor's predicted behaviour. Ra's rule was that a Manifestor does not act in secret and does not act in isolation, and in the same breath that this does not mean telling the whole world.

## The Manifestor child has a different strategy

Manifestors are the only type whose strategy changes with age. This is not a softened rule for children. It is a second strategy, and Ra taught it as one.

> "The adult manifestor, only manifestors have two variations of what is, in essence, their strategy. The manifestor child within the house of its parents always needing to ask permission. The manifestor adult outside of the environment of its parents, it becomes informing."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

The child's version is to ask permission, and the reason is not obedience. A child living in its parents' home genuinely cannot just act, and finds this out early.

> "The generator waits to be asked; the manifestor has to ask. And what it asks for as a child in the house of its parents is permission."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

Ra called the whole territory manners, etiquette, correctness in the formal sense. What asking does for the child is the part most often missed. It is not a restriction on the child's power. It is the only way the child gets to feel that power working.

> "For the manifestor child, this is the only key. It allows them to see something very important about the nature of their power: nobody will get in the way of the release of their power if they're given permission to use it. The manifestor child can find its power that way, does not feel restricted."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

Ra added that this has to be taught from a very early age. If what the child wants is reasonable the answer is usually yes, and the child learns that announcing an intention clears the road rather than starting a fight. The Definitive Book of Human Design adds the exception: a polite Manifestor child who asks will usually be allowed to do what they want, except where safety is at stake.

Without it the pattern runs the other way. Because the aura repels, parents grow concerned, and these children end up heavily controlled and regularly punished, not usually by corporal punishment but by restriction and limitation. The Definitive Book notes that excessive control forces them to rebel or to go passive, and that both repress their power. Ra's account is that such a child develops a deep anger, and that nobody explains to it why.

> "You know, if you have a manifestor child, teach them to be polite, teach them to ask permission. It is a technique that will save them. And if you're a manifestor adult, your strategy is to inform, to inform before you act, to inform."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

Ra was clear that asking is not confined to the parents. It also shapes how the child operates in the classroom and socially, in a room where most of the other children are Generators and Projectors who connect easily to each other and not to this one. The handover to informing comes with leaving the parents' home.

## What anger is telling a Manifestor

Anger is the Manifestor [[Not-Self]] theme, and it is specific: an initiating force meeting an environment that was not told it was coming. Contemporary parenting teaching treats it as a diagnostic rather than a mood. When a Manifestor is angry, the question worth asking is who did not get informed.

Left alone it does not stay at the surface. Ra described not-self Manifestors as fundamentally operating in secret, riding the anger while it slowly eats away at them. The Definitive Book has it coming out one of two ways: as rage and rebelliousness, or as passivity and accommodation. The passive version produces a Manifestor who behaves like a [[Generator]], waiting instead of initiating, and ends up an impulsive workaholic accomplishing little.

## Why Manifestors resist informing

The conditioning that argues against informing is consistent. It says the step is weak, because a powerful being should not have to announce itself. It says it is redundant, because the Manifestor already knows what it is doing. And it says it is dangerous, because a lifetime of being controlled has taught that telling people is how you get stopped.

What grows out of that is avoidance rather than defiance: doing the thing quietly and hoping nobody notices. It does not work, because the aura is felt anyway and the questions come anyway. Ra's claim for the opposite move is the strongest he makes for the strategy:

> "That peacefulness arises out of informing, because informing clears the path. It moves mountains."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

[[Signature|Peace]] is not a personality trait. It is what is left when the resistance stops.

## At work and in partnership

The Definitive Book gives the workplace case. A Manifestor sees a way out of a dead-end job mid-morning, packs up her office, leaves a letter of resignation and walks out. The boss is left without cover and colleagues wonder what they did to offend her. The decision was not wrong and needed nobody's input. What was missing was the notice that would have let everyone adjust, and that might have won her support instead of resistance.

In partnership it is smaller and far more frequent. Ra's example is a Manifestor who gets up mid-evening, walks out and is gone without a word, returning to a partner who has imagined every explanation and will not believe the real one. Ra held that many relationship problems involving Manifestors trace to exactly this.

## Inform inside the Manifesting Generator strategy

A [[Manifesting Generator]] informs second, not first: something appears, the sacral answers, and only then does the Manifesting Generator inform and move. See [[Respond]] for the first step. Skipping the informing lays anger on top of the Generator's frustration, most visibly when they change direction mid-project without telling anyone. Ra warned about the reverse error too, the Manifesting Generator who never waits at all:

> "And a manifesting generator, instead of waiting to respond, tries to play out the role of manifester and meets in resistance an anger that doesn't belong to their form. And it is an anger that could be deeply, deeply debilitating."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

## The practice

Informing is about how to engage, not what to engage in. The decision itself belongs to the Manifestor's [[Inner Authority]], which may be [[Emotional Authority|emotional]], [[Splenic Authority|splenic]] or [[Ego Authority|ego]].

- Decide through your authority first.
- Say what you are about to do, to the people whose lives or plans it changes.
- Do not ask, justify, negotiate or apologise.
- Then act, without waiting for a reply.

> "To all the manifestors out there: you inform. Everything is transformed, just like that."
> -- Ra Uru Hu
