# Mind vs Body Decision-Making

Human Design's central claim is that the mind is not equipped to make decisions for the person it lives in. [[Strategy Overview|Strategy]] and [[Inner Authority Overview|Inner Authority]] exist to take decision-making out of the mind and route it through the body instead. Why the mind fails at this one job, and what the body does that the mind cannot, is the ground beneath all four strategies.

> "The mind is never your Authority. The mind is a passenger, a storyteller, a commentator. It's not here to make decisions."
> -- What is Inner Authority?, Jovian Archive

## Two Authorities, Not One

Human Design never asks anyone to get rid of their mind. It gives the mind a different office.

> "Human design talks about two kinds of authority. Inner authority and outer authority."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

Inner Authority is the body's decision-making mechanism, grounded in what is consistently defined in a chart. Outer authority is the mind. Ra's account of why the two collapsed into one is historical: in the seven-centred form that preceded the nine-centred one, inner and outer authority sat in the same place, mentally, so the mind both made the decisions and did the talking. The inheritance is a mind that assumes the decision is its business.

## What the Mind Is Actually For

Outer authority is not a consolation prize. It is the mind at full power in the one direction it is built for: measurement. Ra traced the *ma* in *maya* to a Sanskrit root meaning to measure.

> "Everything about the nature of mind is measurement. This is the way in which we deal with duality of the world."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

The distinction is directional: the mind's product is for other people.

> "You know, the idealized expression of mind is outer authority. What I share with you, what I offer to you, my outer authority, my mind can be very useful to you. It is not useful to me."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

In practice, outer authority looks like teaching, advising, framing a problem, putting words to something another person is living but has not said. Each is a measurement offered outward, for the other person's Authority to decide on. The same mind deciding what its own body will commit to is that faculty pointed the wrong way.

## How the Open Centres Build the Mind's Criteria

The failure is not that the mind reasons badly. It reasons correctly, from material that was never the person's own.

The Definitive Book of Human Design describes conditioning as unavoidable rather than unfortunate. Undefined channels, gates and centres are continuously filled in by the definition of others, and by the time a child reaches seven years of age, several layers of familial and societal conditioning have been piled over the true self. Conditioning cannot be circumvented or escaped, and is not inherently a bad thing. What makes it a decision-making problem is the next step: absorbing those frequencies puts pressure on the openness and triggers the mind into thinking about all it is missing.

> "It is through your openness that you meet the other. So your mind is based entirely on the conditioning that you receive from other human beings."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

> "The way the not-self operates, the way normalcy operates, is that what we are not determines how we think. What we are not determines what we think."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

Every [[Open Centers|open centre]] carries a not-self theme, and the theme does not stay a feeling. It hardens into a mental strategy, and that strategy becomes a standing condition for saying yes. An open [[Heart Center]] that does not feel worthy will find reasons to take the work, accept the lesser role, carry the burden, because agreeing looks like proof of worth. An open [[G Center]] hunting for direction will find reasons that this person must be the right one. None of that material originates in the person holding it.

So the list of pros and cons is not neutral. It weighs criteria nobody chose, applies them faithfully, and returns an answer that leads back into the [[Not-Self]].

> "You get caught in the trap of thinking that the measurement itself is a way of actually coming to a point of recognition of what is correct for oneself. It's not so."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

## The Time Problem

Even a mind running on clean criteria would get the timing wrong, because it assumes one clock and there are two. Approximately 51 percent of human beings have a defined [[Solar Plexus Center|Solar Plexus]], a figure Jovian Archive's beginner material rounds to about 50 percent; the remaining 49 percent do not.

> "Unemotional beings are here fundamentally to operate in the now. Their process is oriented to the existential. They're here to deal with things spontaneously."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

> "But if you're an emotional being, you're operating in a different time. It's a very different time. Yet, most emotional human beings don't pay any attention to the fact that they have a different natural timing, but try to emulate the timing of those that don't have a defined emotional system."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

For an emotional being there is no truth in the now. For an unemotional being there is only the now, and if nothing comes in the moment there is no answer yet. Ra called these two kinds of beings on the planet, determined by the way in which they are meant to make decisions as themselves. Conditioning pushes each onto the other's clock.

> "And because we are all susceptible to conditioning, those that are unemotional agonize over their decisions and those that are emotional are always jumping."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

How each clock is read belongs to [[Emotional Authority]] and the other Authority pages, not here.

## What the Body Offers Instead

Each Authority is a mechanical signal of the person's own definition meeting circumstance. None is reasoning, and none runs through the open centres:

- **[[Sacral Authority|Sacral]]** -- a gut response in the moment to what is in front of the person.
- **[[Emotional Authority|Emotional]]** -- clarity that accumulates across the wave, never inside one moment of it.
- **[[Splenic Authority|Splenic]]** -- one quiet signal of safety in the now, which does not repeat.
- **[[Ego Authority|Ego]]** -- what the will actually wants, heard in what the person says out loud.
- **[[Self-Projected Authority|Self-projected]]** -- direction recognised by hearing oneself speak.
- **[[Lunar Authority|Lunar]]** -- a full cycle of sampling before anything important settles.
- **[[Mental Authority|Mental]]** -- no inner signal, so the process runs through trusted people and the right environment.

## Mind-Led and Body-Led

- **Mind-led:** weighs pros and cons. **Body-led:** Strategy first, then Authority.
- **Mind-led:** asks which option is objectively best. **Body-led:** asks what this body responds to.
- **Mind-led:** decides in the moment, the mind's only tense. **Body-led:** decides on the body's timing, whether that is now, a wave, or a lunar cycle.
- **Mind-led:** anchors in criteria absorbed through the open centres. **Body-led:** anchors in what is consistently defined.
- **Mind-led:** produces the not-self theme. **Body-led:** produces the signature state.
- **Mind-led:** must believe the conclusion is right before acting. **Body-led:** needs no belief, only the results.
- **Mind-led:** having decided, the mind must defend the decision. **Body-led:** the mind may comment freely, because nothing rests on it.

## The Experiment and Its Timeline

Human Design is offered as an experiment rather than a doctrine. The proposition is not that the body is wise in some mystical sense. It is narrower and testable: run decisions through Strategy and Authority for a meaningful stretch of time, and judge from the results.

The seven-year figure rests on one claim, repeated across Jovian Archive's material and in Ra's lectures: it takes about seven years for every cell in the body to renew itself, so full [[Deconditioning]] is a cellular process rather than a mental decision. The Human Design Dictionary describes seven years of deconditioning, after which the purpose of the incarnation can begin to be experienced. Ra knew the number was unwelcome.

> "Awareness does not emerge in a moment. It doesn't. It's something that matures with the correctness of a being."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

What arrives sooner is feedback, not completion. Ra described the not-self theme as what is present when a being operates out of its mental authority, and the signature as what emerges when it abandons mental authority. That pairing is the one measurement the mind is asked to take: less frustration, anger, bitterness or disappointment, more satisfaction, peace, success or surprise. It is a [[Signature|signpost]], readable long before seven years are up.

Strategy and Authority take a person out of mental decision-making; they do not switch off [[Conditioning]]. The influence keeps arriving. It simply stops being the thing that decides.

## What the Mind Becomes

Living correctly does not quiet the mind.

> "My mind has never stopped measuring. It's never stopped trying to suggest to me that there's something that I should or should not do. It's what mind does."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

The change is in the mind's authority, not its volume. Once it is not responsible for the outcome, the same commentary stops being a burden and becomes something a person can watch.

> "We all think about things that don't matter. But it doesn't mean you make them matter and you do something about them. Because your mind is not here to decide. And as long as your mind doesn't decide, you're free."
> -- Ra Uru Hu

This is the recovery move at the centre of the system: not the defeat of the mind, but the end of its second job.

> "Let your mind do all the measuring it wants to do. But save the decision-making for yourself, for the essence of what is you, so that you can move along a track that allows you to fulfill your purpose here."
> -- Ra Uru Hu
